## Premium vs generic ChatGPT-basic

As Experio Labs (2026) and Tech Talk (2026) document, AI-generated proposals fail because of the "Fluency Trap": smooth, interchangeable cliches ("elevate your digital presence," "seamless user experience," "unlock your brand's potential") that strip out proof of effort.

DealNote already has the hardest ingredient to fake: real, measured website diagnostics (exact load times, missing alt counts, 404 links, absent LocalBusiness schema). To make every template feel premium, anchor every section in that evidence:
- Evidence-first openers: open with the prospect's exact measured finding ("Your homepage took 2.8s on mobile and 1 internal link returns a 404") instead of generic flattery.
- Explicit "Not included" boundaries.
- Plain-English business impact without invented math: tie slow mobile load or tiny tap-to-call buttons to missed calls and abandoned quotes, without fabric[...].
- Visual hierarchy over plain-text walls: Better Proposals reports an 82% conversion lift for visually structured proposals over plain text, and Proposify shows customizing at least 30% of the executive summary meaningfully lifts close rates.
- Zero AI buzzword vocabulary: ban "delve," "elevate," "robust," "synergy," "game-changer," "[...]" from every static template string.

## Compliance guardrails

Split between editable commercial fields and locked compliance guardrails, grounded in FTC and FCC rules.

- Pricing and Packages: default blank $____ placeholders, never pre-fill suggested market rates or fake "original vs. discount" anchor prices. Editable: dollar amounts, currency symbol, tier names, tier count (1-3), payment split (e.g. 50/50), and vali[...]. Basis: prevents deceptive reference-price claims.
- Performance and ROI Claims: ban pre-written revenue guarantees, ranking promises ("#1 on Google"), or invented dollar-loss calculators. Show inline reminder: "Cite measured audit facts only." Editable: plain-English description of the business problem and factual audit numbers. Basis: FTC Advertising Substantiation Policy and Section 5 (15 U.S.C. section 45).
- Testimonials and Case Studies: never pre-populate sample client quotes or fictional success metrics. Keep slot empty ([Add your real past project]) and toggleable off. Editable: freelancer's own real client name (with permission), real project URL, verifiable outcome, or toggle the About / Proof section off. Basis: FTC Final Rule 16 CFR Part 465.
- Cold Email Footer and Headers: keep CAN-SPAM footer structure visible by default (physical postal address slot + clear opt-out line [...]) and never pre-fill deceptive "Re:"/"Fwd:" subject lines. Editable: sender's real name, studio brand, valid physical postal address, opt-out wording. Basis: FTC CAN-SPAM Compliance Guide: no B2B exception; up to $53,088 per violating email; opt-out must work 30 days or more and be honored withi[...].
- Channel Scope (no cold SMS): keep cold outreach templates strictly email-only. Retain the explicit "No cold SMS scripts" boundary and international consent note (CASL/GDPR/PECR). Editable: optional warm-lead follow-up text only if the user checks "Prospect requested contact", or keep SMS o[...]. Basis: TCPA (47 U.S.C. section 227) and 2026 TCPA Guide: marketing SMS requires prior express written consent; statutory damages $500-$1,500 per unsolici[...].
- Contract and Terms Section: retain a visible UI note: "Starting terms outline, not legal advice. Have a lawyer review before binding use." Editable: payment milestones, revision caps, late-fee policy, IP transfer timing, cancellation terms, plus [...]. Basis: prevents unauthorized-practice-of-law perception.

## What not to build

- No Cold SMS Scripts or Auto-Dialers: TCPA statutory penalties ($500-$1,500 per text).
- No E-Signature, Stripe Checkout, or CRM: legally auditable e-signatures, invoice billing, or pipeline CRMs create ongoing server an[...] - keep acceptance simple: reply-to-accept or a "Next Steps" section.
- No 100+ Thin Industry Clones: 80 nearly identical templates that only swap "Plumber" for "Roofer" feels like filler. Six [...] niche overlays with real vocabulary swaps are enough.
- No Open-Ended "AI Write Everything" Mode: unguarded LLM generation reintroduces hallucinations, invented ROI stats, and ChatGPT cliches.
- No Email Sending Engine or Open-Tracking Pixels: sending email from DealNote creates domain-reputation headaches; Reply.io's 2026 data shows campaigns with open tracking turned off earn nearly 2x the replies while Apple Mail inflates o[...].
- No Drag-and-Drop Figma Canvas: freelancers buy DealNote to finish a pitch in 5 minutes, not spend 45 minutes nudging boxes.
